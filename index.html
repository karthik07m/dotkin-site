Meet DOT

A companion that keeps time, not tabs.

DOT is a small focus device with display eyes. It follows your cursor, blinks, and rests its eyes when you pause. Pick its body, colour and something to wear once, then it stays out of the way. Click it and it shows exactly what matters right now.

  • Nothing running: today's three priorities, each with its own play button.
  • Mid-block: time left in large type, plus Done, Pause and +5.
  • Just finished: Again, Something else, or Stop for now.
  • Always: Park a thought. Type it, press Enter, the block keeps running.
  • Always: Ask. A question turns the bubble into a small chat with Lifebot.
New in 1.0.3 and later

Ask it. And let it poke you.

Ask DOT turns the bubble into a small chat. Your questions and its answers stay in a thread, Enter sends, and it answers from your live data in bubble-sized lines: "what's still pending?", "should I take a break?". It is the same Lifebot as the app's tab. Open in Lifebot continues the thread in the app.

The poke. Once there are three logged days, if your usual first block is thirty minutes overdue with nothing logged, DOT hops, chimes softly, opens its bubble on its own and looks expectant: one line for the day above your priorities with their play buttons. Start a block within five minutes and it cheers. Dismiss it and it waits twenty minutes, glances at you, then gets bored and yawns. Once a day, never after 2pm, and an ignored bubble folds itself away.

The Map

See where your hours actually went.

A live graph of everything you've planned, shown here on a real profile with three projects and ten hours logged. Each project keeps to its own side. Hover a node to trace its branch, drag one and the rest follows on springs. The running block pulses and the path to it lights up.

  • Click a project or section to fold it.
  • Click an open task to start a block on it.
  • Pan, pinch, or Ctrl+scroll to zoom. Reset layout replays the opening.
Focus mode

Two buttons. That's the whole interface while you work.

Starting a block pins that task to the Focus strip, so the strip and the timer always agree about what you're doing. When the block ends the app asks one line: where did you stop? The next block on that task shows it as Last time.

  • Breaks, stopping early, energy and +5/+10 wait behind the … menu.
  • Deleting a task is one click, with Undo in the toast.
  • Closing the window hides to the tray. A running timer keeps counting.
The Log

Your day, written down for you.

The Log tab opens with today's note: the thoughts you kept and a reflection box. Only today's file is ever rewritten, so anything you edit in past days is safe. Point the notes folder at an Obsidian vault to link and search there.

Lifebot · optional · bring your own key

A coach that argues back with your own numbers.

Add an Anthropic API key and Claude reads your actual log: blocks, tasks, streaks, what's carried over. No generic productivity advice. If your setup is wrong, it says so.

  1. Morning briefing. Before you pick today's three, one read on the shape of the day: what carried over, what's pending, what's gone stale.
  2. Proactive check-ins. On open, after a block, when priorities lock. What's pending, the single biggest risk, one specific suggestion. Ninety words, tops.
  3. Chat when you want it. Sequence my day, help me choose a task, or talk a stuck afternoon through. It knows the data, so it can push back.
  4. Ask from the desktop. The same Lifebot inside DOT's bubble, told to keep answers short. The poke's morning line is written from your numbers too.

One file. Yours. Readable without the app.

No account to create, no server to trust. Dotkin writes a single JSON file on your machine and shows you exactly where it is.

Questions

Short answers.

Wasn't this called Focus Deck?

Yes, until September 2026. Same app, new name. Existing data, the Drive export folder and the settings file keep their old names on purpose, so nothing breaks when you update.

Is it really free?

Yes. No account, no subscription, no telemetry. The only thing that can cost money is the optional Lifebot chat, which uses your own Anthropic API key and is billed to you by Anthropic. Typical coaching chats cost cents.

Will DOT nag me?

Gently. The poke, the one that opens the bubble and chimes, fires at most once a day, only after three days of history, only when your usual start is thirty minutes overdue, and never after 2pm. Later dismisses it. Between blocks, DOT drops a short line above its head every half hour or so during the day: what's pinned, what's next, how long since your last block. It fades in twelve seconds, and touching the pet or the app resets the clock. Ask and the poke's written line need the optional API key; the poke and the nudges don't.

Does it need the internet?

No. Tracking, timers, the planner, the map, notes and insights all work offline. Only Lifebot talks to a server, and only when you send it a message.

What happens when I close the window?

It hides to the tray so a running timer keeps counting. Quit properly from the tray menu. Open at login can be turned off from the tray or in Settings.

Can I use it with Obsidian?

Yes. Settings has a Daily notes folder. Point it at your vault and each day's note appears there as plain markdown, linkable and searchable like any other note.

Is this a Pomodoro app?

Close, but not dogmatic. Blocks are 10 to 45 minutes, breaks are optional, and running past the planned time is treated as a win rather than a mistake.

Windows says "Windows protected your PC". Is it safe?

Yes. The installer isn't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen shows that screen for any new app. Click More info, then Run anyway. It happens once. Intel Mac builds aren't published yet; run from source in about a minute.
